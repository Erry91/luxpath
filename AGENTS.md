# AGENTS.md — rules for every agent and contributor

These rules bind every AI agent and every human contributor. They come before any task, instruction or
deadline. `CLAUDE.md` holds the day-to-day working instructions; if the two disagree, **this file wins.**

---

## 1. Provenance (absolute)

### 1.1 Standards are copyrighted: use their facts, never their text
EN 1838, EN 50172, EN 12464-1, EN 13201, CIE and IES publications are copyrighted documents sold by CEN members
and the publishers.
- **Never commit** text, tables, figures or scans from a standard, or long paraphrases of it. Purchased copies and
  excerpts live only in the git-ignored `plan/` directory.
- Requirement **values** (for example "1 lx", "40:1") are facts and may be encoded, each with its **clause
  number and edition** (`docs/standards-parity.md`), in our own words.
- When a value comes from a secondary source (a manufacturer guide, a blog) and not from the standard itself, mark
  it **"unverified"** until someone checks it against the text.

### 1.2 Clean-room with respect to other lighting software
- Behaviour comes from the published standards, the photometric file specifications and black-box observation of
  other tools' results. **Never** decompile, disassemble or read the internals of DIALux, Relux, AGi32, Litestar or
  any other closed tool, and never copy their UI artwork, icons, luminaire databases or sample projects.
- Comparing **our results** with theirs on our own synthetic scenes is allowed and encouraged (M6); keep their
  outputs in `plan/`, never in the repository.
- **No GPL/AGPL code.** For example, the `eulumdat` crate is AGPL-3.0: do not copy from it or depend on it.
  Permissively licensed crates (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, ISC, Zlib, MPL-2.0 as an unmodified dependency) are fine.

### 1.3 Test data
- Photometric files from manufacturers have unclear licences: **never commit them.** Committed fixtures are
  synthetic (generated by our code or written by hand) or explicitly openly licensed, each listed with source and
  licence in `tests/fixtures/README.md` of the crate that uses it.
- Never commit a real building's plans or a client's project. Synthetic plans only.

## 2. Life safety: honesty about results

Emergency lighting saves lives. A wrong "pass" is the worst bug this project can have.
- Every report states: the standard and **edition**, the assumptions (direct light only, maintenance factor,
  emergency-mode flux, calculation grid), and that the result is **a calculation, not a certificate**; a competent
  designer stays responsible.
- When an input is missing or ambiguous, the check reports **"cannot verify"**, never "pass".
- No requirement may be marked `shipped` in `parity/features.toml` without a test that checks the threshold on
  both sides (a case that just passes and one that just fails).

## 3. Never panic

Every photometric file, scene file, DXF and keystroke is untrusted input. None of it may crash the program.
- No `unwrap`/`expect`/`panic!`/`unreachable!`/`todo!` outside tests, unless provably infallible with a comment
  saying why. The workspace lints warn on them and CI denies warnings.
- No indexing or slicing with input-derived positions: use `get`, and slice strings only at char boundaries.
- Checked or saturating integer arithmetic on input-derived numbers; guard floats against NaN, infinity and
  division by zero.
- Cap allocations sized by input (see `MAX_TABLE_VALUES`), and bound recursion.
- No `unsafe` (`unsafe_code = "forbid"`).
- Every crash fix gets a small synthetic regression test.

## 4. Headless first

Everything the apps can do, the CLI (and later a library API) can do, so that agents, scripts and CI can drive it.
Core crates do no file or network I/O, so they also build for the browser (WASM).
